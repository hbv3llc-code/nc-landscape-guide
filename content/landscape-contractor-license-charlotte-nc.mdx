## State License Applies in Charlotte

North Carolina landscape contractor licensing operates entirely at the state level under Chapter 89D of the NC General Statutes. The NC Landscape Contractors' Registration Board issues a single registration that is valid for landscape contracting work throughout the state — Charlotte does not have a separate city-level landscape contractor license.

If your work in Charlotte falls under the scope of landscape contracting as defined by Chapter 89D — including landscape installation, grading, irrigation, and certain hardscape work — you are required to hold a current NCLCRB registration. This applies whether you are a sole proprietor doing residential work in South End or a larger firm handling commercial contracts in the University City corridor.

## Requirements for Charlotte Contractors

The licensing requirements for Charlotte contractors are the statewide requirements established by the NCLCRB. You will need to meet minimum experience requirements, pass the state landscape contractor examination, maintain the required insurance coverage, and pay registration and renewal fees as set by the board.

Charlotte's size means there is a competitive landscape contracting market, and holding your NCLCRB license is an important differentiator when bidding on commercial or larger residential projects. Contractors preparing for the exam should review plant identification, landscape design principles, installation methods, and business law topics covered in the state exam. Contact the NCLCRB for current exam site locations serving the Charlotte metro area.

## Working in Mecklenburg County

Mecklenburg County and the Charlotte metro area present some of the most diverse landscape contracting opportunities in North Carolina. The region's Piedmont location gives it a climate similar to Raleigh, but Charlotte's urban core creates a pronounced heat island effect — temperatures in dense commercial areas can run several degrees warmer than surrounding suburbs, which influences plant selection and irrigation needs.

Charlotte's rapid suburban expansion into communities like Ballantyne, Steele Creek, and the Lake Norman area to the north keeps new residential construction demand consistently high. At the same time, the city's Uptown core and surrounding mixed-use districts generate steady commercial landscape maintenance and installation contracts. Common project types include turf renovation, ornamental bed installation, hardscape patios and pool surrounds, commercial site enhancement, and irrigation design. The red clay soils throughout much of Mecklenburg County require thoughtful drainage and soil amendment work.

## Local Permits and Regulations

While your NCLCRB registration authorizes landscape contracting throughout North Carolina, Charlotte and Mecklenburg County maintain their own permitting systems for certain types of work. These local requirements exist independently of your state license.

The Charlotte-Mecklenburg Storm Water Services program has active requirements related to land-disturbing activities and impervious surface. Retaining walls above specified heights, grading projects that disturb more than a threshold area of land, and irrigation systems with backflow prevention components may all require permits from Charlotte's Development Center. Charlotte also enforces tree canopy and landscape buffer requirements on commercial projects. Before breaking ground on any project, verify current permit requirements directly with the Charlotte Development Center or Mecklenburg County — requirements change and project-specific review may be needed.

## Getting Started in Charlotte

Beginning the licensing process for landscape contractor work in Charlotte starts with confirming your eligibility under NCLCRB rules. The board sets experience and documentation requirements that you should review carefully before applying. Once you are ready, contact the NCLCRB directly to request current application materials, exam schedules, and fee information.
