## State License Applies in Wilmington

North Carolina landscape contractor licensing is a statewide credential administered by the NCLCRB under Chapter 89D of the NC General Statutes. There is no separate Wilmington city license or New Hanover County license for landscape contractors. Your NCLCRB registration, once issued, is valid for qualifying landscape contracting work throughout the entire state, including Wilmington and the Cape Fear region.

If you are performing work in Wilmington that falls within the scope of Chapter 89D — landscape installation, grading, irrigation, hardscape work, and similar activities — you must hold a current NCLCRB registration. This applies to contractors working on residential properties in established neighborhoods as well as those handling large commercial or waterfront development projects.

## Requirements for Wilmington Contractors

The requirements to earn your NCLCRB landscape contractor registration are uniform across North Carolina. You must meet the board's field experience requirements, pass the state landscape contractor examination, maintain required liability insurance, and pay registration and renewal fees on schedule.

The NCLCRB exam tests knowledge across plant identification, landscape installation practices, grading and drainage, irrigation systems, and business and legal topics. For Wilmington-area contractors, knowing coastal-adapted plants — including salt-tolerant species suited to maritime exposures — is a practical advantage in local work even if the exam itself applies statewide standards. Contact the NCLCRB directly for current exam site locations serving southeastern North Carolina. Cape Fear Community College offers relevant horticulture programs that can supplement your exam preparation.

## Working in New Hanover County

New Hanover County sits on the Cape Fear Coast and encompasses Wilmington along with beach communities including Wrightsville Beach, Carolina Beach, and Kure Beach. The region has a coastal subtropical climate — warm temperatures, high humidity, mild winters, and a long growing season that extends well into fall. However, working in this coastal environment brings considerations that inland contractors do not typically face.

Salt spray and salt-laden soils near the ocean and Intracoastal Waterway limit plant selection and require that contractors understand which species tolerate maritime exposure. Projects near the water must be designed with durability and salt tolerance in mind. Hurricane season, running June through November, is a real consideration for landscape installation timing and for selecting plants and structures that can withstand high-wind events. Wilmington's strong residential and short-term rental markets, combined with significant commercial and hospitality development along the coast, create broad and steady demand for licensed contractors who can navigate these conditions skillfully.

## Local Permits and Regulations

Your NCLCRB registration authorizes landscape contracting work throughout North Carolina but does not substitute for local permitting requirements in Wilmington and New Hanover County. Coastal locations add a layer of regulatory complexity that is worth understanding before you begin work.

In addition to standard local permits for land-disturbing activity, retaining walls, and irrigation connections, work near coastal waters, wetlands, or the Intracoastal Waterway may fall under the jurisdiction of the NC Division of Coastal Management (CAMA) — the Coastal Area Management Act program. CAMA permits or exemption determinations may be required for grading, fill, or vegetation installation within designated Areas of Environmental Concern. The City of Wilmington Development Services Department and New Hanover County Planning and Land Use are your primary local contacts for permit requirements. Always verify requirements before beginning any project, particularly in areas near water.
