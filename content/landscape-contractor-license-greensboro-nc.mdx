## State License Applies in Greensboro

North Carolina landscape contractor licensing is a state-level system governed by Chapter 89D of the NC General Statutes. The NCLCRB issues a registration that is valid for qualifying landscape contracting work anywhere in North Carolina — there is no separate Greensboro city license or Guilford County license for this profession.

If you are performing landscape contracting work in Greensboro that falls within the scope of Chapter 89D, you are required to hold a current NCLCRB registration. This covers activities such as landscape installation, grading, ornamental planting, irrigation, and associated hardscape construction. Operating without a required license exposes you to civil penalties and can affect your ability to obtain licensure in the future.

## Requirements for Greensboro Contractors

The requirements for landscape contractors working in Greensboro are the same statewide standards established by the NCLCRB. You will need to document qualifying field experience, pass the state landscape contractor examination, maintain adequate liability insurance coverage, and keep your registration current through timely renewals and associated fees.

Greensboro is home to several higher education institutions and has a strong vocational training tradition. Contractors preparing for the NCLCRB exam may find relevant coursework through Guilford Technical Community College and area programs. The exam covers plant identification, landscape installation practices, grading and drainage fundamentals, irrigation principles, and business and legal knowledge. Check with the NCLCRB for current approved exam sites in the Piedmont Triad region.

## Working in Guilford County

Guilford County encompasses Greensboro, High Point, and surrounding communities, offering a landscape market that is relatively balanced between residential and commercial work. Greensboro's four-season Piedmont climate — with warm, humid summers, colorful falls, and winters that can bring occasional ice and snow — shapes planting choices and seasonal service schedules in ways that differ from coastal or mountain markets.

Established neighborhoods like Fisher Park, Irving Park, and Sunset Hills present strong demand for landscape renovation, mature tree care coordination, and high-quality hardscape work. The city's commercial districts, medical facilities, and college campuses provide ongoing institutional landscape contracts. High Point's reputation as a furniture industry hub brings corporate facility landscape work to the county as well. Soils in Guilford County are predominantly clay-based Piedmont types that require careful drainage and amendment planning, particularly for turf establishment and ornamental beds.

## Local Permits and Regulations

Your NCLCRB registration is your state-level credential for landscape contracting, but it does not eliminate local permitting requirements in Greensboro and Guilford County. These are separate processes administered by local government offices.

The City of Greensboro may require permits for land-disturbing activities above certain thresholds, retaining walls over specified heights, and irrigation system connections. Greensboro has an active stormwater management program, and projects involving grading, drainage alterations, or impervious surface additions near streams or in designated watershed areas may be subject to review. Guilford County also has its own permitting authority for work in unincorporated portions of the county. Before starting any project, contact the Greensboro Development Services Department or Guilford County Planning and Development to confirm which permits apply.

## Getting Started in Greensboro

To pursue your NC landscape contractor license for work in Greensboro, begin by confirming your eligibility under NCLCRB rules. The board specifies experience requirements and documentation you will need to provide with your application. Contact the NCLCRB to obtain current application forms, confirm exam schedules, and review the fee structure.
