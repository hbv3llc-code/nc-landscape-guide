## State License Applies in Durham

North Carolina landscape contractor licensing is a statewide system established under Chapter 89D of the NC General Statutes and administered by the NCLCRB. There is no separate Durham city license or Durham County license for landscape contractors — the state registration is the credential you need, and it is valid for qualifying landscape contracting work anywhere in North Carolina, including Durham.

If your landscape contracting work in Durham falls within the activities covered by Chapter 89D — such as landscape installation, grading, irrigation, or hardscape construction — you must hold a current NCLCRB registration. This requirement applies to contractors of all sizes, from solo operators serving Durham's many residential neighborhoods to firms handling large commercial or institutional projects.

## Requirements for Durham Contractors

The licensing requirements for contractors working in Durham are the uniform statewide standards set by the NCLCRB. To obtain your registration you must demonstrate qualifying field experience in landscape contracting, pass the NCLCRB landscape contractor examination, carry appropriate liability insurance, and pay applicable registration fees. Renewals are required periodically to keep your license active.

Durham's proximity to Raleigh means Triangle-area contractors may have access to exam sites and NCLCRB resources serving both markets. Check directly with the NCLCRB for current exam locations and scheduling. Duke University and North Carolina Central University have campuses in Durham, and local community college programs can provide supplementary preparation for the state exam, particularly in plant science and horticultural practices relevant to Piedmont growing conditions.

## Working in Durham County

Durham County offers a landscape contracting market shaped by rapid residential growth, a robust renovation market in established neighborhoods, and significant institutional demand. The city has experienced dramatic revitalization over the past two decades, with older neighborhoods like Burch Avenue, Old West Durham, and Northgate Park seeing sustained interest in landscape upgrades alongside new construction. The Research Triangle Park nearby adds commercial and corporate campus landscape work to the mix.

The climate in Durham is humid subtropical and closely mirrors that of neighboring Raleigh — hot, humid summers with temperatures regularly reaching the upper 90s, mild winters with occasional ice events, and a long growing season that supports a wide range of ornamental and turfgrass species. Soils across Durham County vary but include significant areas of clay-heavy Piedmont soils. Durham's growing interest in sustainable landscaping, native plants, and low-water-use designs creates opportunities for contractors who can offer those specializations alongside traditional installation services.

## Local Permits and Regulations

Your NCLCRB registration authorizes you to perform landscape contracting work throughout North Carolina, but it does not replace local permitting requirements specific to Durham. These local requirements are administered separately by city and county offices.

The City of Durham may require permits for land-disturbing activity above threshold acreages, retaining walls above certain heights, and irrigation connections to the public water system. Durham's stormwater program has requirements that can affect grading and drainage projects, particularly in watershed protection areas and near streams. The county's Unified Development Ordinance also includes landscape and buffer requirements for commercial and multifamily development. Before starting work, check with the Durham Development Services Department or Durham County Planning to confirm which permits and approvals are needed for your specific project.

## Getting Started in Durham

Pursuing your NC landscape contractor license to work in Durham begins with confirming that you meet the NCLCRB's eligibility requirements. Review the board's experience documentation requirements carefully and contact the NCLCRB directly to get current application materials, exam schedules, and fee information.
