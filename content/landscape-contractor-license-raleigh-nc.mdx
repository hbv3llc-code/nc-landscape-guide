## State License Applies in Raleigh

North Carolina landscape contractor licensing is administered at the state level under Chapter 89D of the NC General Statutes. There is no separate Raleigh city license for landscape contractors — the NCLCRB registration is the credential that authorizes you to perform landscape contracting work anywhere in North Carolina, including Raleigh and the broader Triangle area.

If you are operating a landscape contracting business in Raleigh and your work involves activities covered under Chapter 89D — such as grading, landscape installation, irrigation, or hardscape construction — you are required to hold a valid NCLCRB license regardless of project size. Working without this license can result in civil penalties and loss of the ability to obtain future licensure.

## Requirements for Raleigh Contractors

The requirements for landscape contractors working in Raleigh are the same state requirements that apply across North Carolina. To obtain your NCLCRB registration you will need to demonstrate qualifying experience in the field, pass the state landscape contractor examination, carry appropriate liability insurance, and pay the applicable registration fees.

The NCLCRB administers examinations at approved testing locations. Raleigh-area contractors should check directly with the NCLCRB for current exam sites available in the Triangle region, as testing locations can change. Wake Technical Community College and other local institutions have historically offered horticulture and landscape-related coursework that candidates find useful when preparing for the exam.

## Working in Wake County

Wake County encompasses Raleigh and a ring of fast-growing suburbs including Cary, Apex, Morrisville, and Knightdale. The region sits in the Carolina Piedmont with a humid subtropical climate — hot and humid summers, mild winters, and a generous growing season that typically runs from early spring through late fall.

Landscape contractors in Wake County work across a broad range of project types. Residential demand is driven by continuous new construction subdivisions alongside established neighborhoods seeking renovations. Commercial work is substantial given Raleigh's role as a state capital and a tech and life sciences hub. Common work includes turf installation and renovation, irrigation system installation, ornamental planting, hardscape patios and walkways, and stormwater-related grading. The clay-heavy soils common in much of Wake County require attention to drainage design and soil amendment practices.

## Local Permits and Regulations

Your NCLCRB license authorizes you to perform landscape contracting work throughout North Carolina, but it does not replace local permitting requirements. The City of Raleigh and Wake County have their own permitting processes that apply to certain types of landscape and site work.

Retaining walls over a certain height, grading or land-disturbing activities above a threshold acreage, and irrigation systems connected to potable water supplies may all require permits from the City of Raleigh Development Services or Wake County. Erosion and sedimentation control permits may also be required for larger land-disturbing projects under state and local rules. Always verify current permit requirements with the Raleigh Development Services Department before beginning work — these local requirements exist independently of your state landscape contractor license.

## Getting Started in Raleigh

If you are ready to pursue your NC landscape contractor license to work in Raleigh, start by verifying that you meet the eligibility requirements set by the NCLCRB, including the experience thresholds. Contact the NCLCRB directly to confirm current application requirements, exam dates, and fees — the board is your official source for all licensing information.

Once you confirm eligibility, submit your application, gather your documentation, and begin preparing for the landscape contractor examination. With Raleigh's continued growth, a valid NCLCRB license positions you to access one of the most active landscape contracting markets in the state.
